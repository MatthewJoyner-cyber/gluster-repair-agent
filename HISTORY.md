# Companion origins before the repository split

This frozen summary describes the workflow from which the companion is
derived. It is not a copied ledger, transcript, or claim of a prior public
agent release. Future implementation detail belongs in commits.

The original local agent combined project steering, focused Gluster skills,
and bounded scripts for inspection, editing, tests, and named operations.
Experience favored narrow reusable helper contracts over unrestricted SSH,
sudo, or large opaque interpreter commands. Trust rules and filesystem access
were treated separately.

Steering was divided into active work, durable findings, and completed proof
records so continuation did not require replaying long conversations. Repair
guidance emphasized independent evidence, explicit authority, arbiter role
safety, preservation, targeted verification, and stopping after unknown writes.
Review tags identified implemented and validated review checkpoints.

Some local instructions depended on specific checkout paths, lab hosts,
personal preferences, and operational artifacts. Those remain private.
This candidate distills the portable rules into five skills, with a small
Codex adapter, blank external-private templates, and a privacy audit.

The old ledger is not included. The core carries its own sanitized implementation
prehistory. Personal steering and incident ledgers remain under the user's home.
Future public changes record behavior, reasoning, validation, and caveats in
their commits, with opt-in maintainer aliases/contact information.

The candidate now includes small, parameterized helpers for inspection,
reviewed patches, privacy audit, candidate inventory, and named local checks.
It does not carry over the full original helper suite, deployment policy,
automatic skill packaging, or established live operational compatibility.
Those need continued qualification before release; they are not implied by
copying skill instructions.
